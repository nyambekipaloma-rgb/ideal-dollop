  <p>The Disappearance That Created The Bermuda Triangle Myth</p>
  <p>5 December 1945 | Fort Lauderdale, Florida | 27 Men Lost</p>

<div class="container">

  <div class="card">
    <h2>1. What Was Flight 19?</h2>
    <p>5 US Navy TBM Avenger torpedo bombers took off for a normal 2-hour navigation training flight. Leader: Lt. Charles Carroll Taylor.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>FT-28 - Lt. Charles Taylor (Leader)</li>
      <li>FT-36 - Capt. Edward Powers</li>
      <li>FT-117 - Ens. Joseph Bossi</li>
      <li>FT-81 - 2nd Lt. Forest Gerber</li>
      <li>FT-3 - Capt. George Stivers</li>
    </ul>
    <p><b>Total:</b> 14 men in Flight 19 + 13 men in rescue plane = 27 men lost.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>2. Minute-By-Minute Timeline</h2>
    <div class="timeline">
      <div><b>2:10 PM</b> - Takeoff from Fort Lauderdale. Weather perfect.</div>
      <div><b>3:40 PM</b> - Taylor: "We can't see land. We seem to be off course."</div>
      <div><b>4:00 PM</b> - Taylor: "Both my compasses are out... I am sure I'm in the Keys but I don't know how far down."</div>
      <div><b>4:25 PM</b> - Taylor decides to fly West - This was CORRECT.</div>
      <div><b>4:30 PM</b> - Taylor changes mind, turns East - FATAL MISTAKE. Flies away from land.</div>
      <div><b>5:50 PM</b> - Powers: "We are completely lost... Even the ocean doesn't look as it should."</div>
      <div><b>6:04 PM</b> - Taylor: "When the first plane drops below 10 gallons, we all go down together."</div>
      <div><b>6:20 PM</b> - Silence. No more radio.</div>
      <div><b>7:50 PM</b> - Rescue plane PBM Mariner explodes. Ship sees fireball + oil slick.</div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>3. Why Was There No Debris?</h2>
    <p>This is not proof of aliens. It is expected:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Avengers were heavy metal - they sink in 30 seconds to 2 minutes.</li>
      <li>They ditched at NIGHT, far from land.</li>
      <li>The Gulf Stream (5-6 km/h) carried any small debris 60-70km away overnight.</li>
      <li>A storm that night broke up oil slicks and debris.</li>
      <li>Search started 12 hours late, over 250,000 sq miles.</li>
    </ul>
    <p><b>Comparison:</b> MH370 (2014) - Modern Boeing 777 with GPS. Took 1.5 years to find ONE piece. We still haven't found the main wreckage.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>4. The 1991 False Discovery</h2>
    <p>In 1991, Graham Hawkes found FIVE Avengers off Fort Lauderdale. One had "28" on it. Everyone thought it was Flight 19.</p>
    <p><b>But:</b> Bureau Numbers did not match. One plane was BuNo 23990 - lost on 9 Oct 1943, crew survived. The other 4 were also different crashes from different years.</p>
    <p><b>Why 5 planes in same place?</b> Fort Lauderdale was a training base. Hundreds of student pilots crashed in the same training area over 4 years. It's like 5 car wrecks at the same dangerous corner - not one crash.</p>
    <p><b>What it proves:</b> If Flight 19 crashed near Florida, we would have found them. Since we didn't, they must have flown much further east, into deep Atlantic.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <h2>5. Theories Tested</h2>
    <div class="theory"><b>1. Human Error (Most Likely):</b> Taylor got lost, thought he was over Keys but was over Bahamas, flew wrong way, ran out of fuel. Supported by radio logs.</div>
    <div class="theory"><b>2. Magnetic Anomaly:</b> US Geological Survey found magnetic field normal. No proof.</div>
    <div class="theory"><b>3. Methane Gas:</b> Real science, but no methane release recorded on Dec 5, 1945.</div>
    <div class="theory"><b>4. Rogue Waves:</b> Real, but Flight 19 was in air, not on water.</div>
    <div class="theory"><b>5. Aliens / Atlantis / Time Warp:</b> Zero physical evidence. Quotes taken out of context.</div>
  </div>

  <div class="verdict">
    CONCLUSION: The absence of debris is not evidence of a supernatural disappearance. It is the expected outcome of a night-time ditching in the Gulf Stream in 1945. Flight 19 was lost due to navigational error, poor leadership, and fuel exhaustion - amplified by media 19 years later when Vincent Gaddis coined "Bermuda Triangle" in 1964.
